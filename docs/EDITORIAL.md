# Editorial: structure, pacing, voice

> DRAFT 2026-09-15, derived from `deliverables/documentary.py` (the seven-section arc and its
> reference bounds), `StoryPlan` (hook ≤ 120 chars, 1–3 sentences per beat), the wind story
> fixture, and the pacing hypotheses in the build brief. Needs the creator's review. Pacing numbers
> are starting ranges to test, not rules; semantic boundaries and comprehension override them.

## Narrative structure (ten-minute reference)

| Section | Purpose | Reference window |
|---|---|---|
| cold_open | The hook: one concrete, surprising, true observation | 0:00–0:20 |
| question_stakes | The specific question and why the viewer should care | 0:20–0:50 |
| build_model | Build the mechanism piece by piece, one entity at a time | 0:50–3:00 |
| run_system | Run the model on real data; the audience watches it work | 3:00–6:30 |
| change_variable | Change one input; show what moves and what does not | 6:30–8:15 |
| show_limits | Where the model breaks, what the data cannot say | 8:15–9:15 |
| synthesis | The answer, restated in one sentence, and what it changes | 9:15–10:00 |

Windows scale proportionally for other lengths. Every episode has a setup in `cold_open` that
pays off in `synthesis`; the episode-level review checks for it.

## Pacing (hypotheses to test)

- Meaningful within-scene change: every 3–8 s. New composition: every 8–20 s. A complex diagram may
  hold 20–40 s or more while it develops. After a dense reveal: 1–3 s of inspection with nothing
  new appearing.
- Never force a cut, a visual-mode quota or novelty for its own sake. Change representation only
  when the new one answers a different question.
- Reading-time floor: a label or quote stays on screen for its reading time (words / 3.3 per
  second, minimum 1.2 s) plus the inspection pause. If it cannot fit, split the scene or replan;
  never compress below the floor.

## Visual continuity

- One main explanatory focus at a time. Progressive disclosure. Labels next to their referents.
- The same entity keeps the same colour, position, scale and label across the episode.
- Charts stay still while the narration is about them; axis and domain changes are shown, not
  slipped in. No overshoot easing on values. Missing data stays a gap.
- Comparisons share a baseline, a unit and a denominator, or the compiler refuses them.

## Presenter voice

- Plain declarative English, short sentences, active verbs (the wind fixture: "Wind beat nuclear.
  Then Sweden stopped building.").
- Numbers spoken with their unit and time basis the first time they appear.
- Uncertainty is said out loud ("about", "at least", "the agency estimates"), never hidden.
- The presenter says only what the creator recorded or approved. No invented experiences, tests,
  product use or opinions. Synthesized sentences carry the creator's wording from the memo or the
  approved script, never new claims.
- Deliberate silence over filler. No canned reactions, no fake laughs, no whoosh per reveal.

## Recording brief (per episode)

Generated from the script: the hook, one consequential observation, the conclusion, each as a
short paragraph the creator reads in their own words, plus an optional unscripted memo for the
angle. Target 60–90 s of original speech in one 5–10 minute session (hypothesis).
